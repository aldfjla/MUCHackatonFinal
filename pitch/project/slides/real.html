Would it work on a real supplier?

What carries over

  • Buying on total cost over the horizon, not unit price
  • Every claim cited from our own data, so a buyer can stand behind the e-mail
  • Package trades: payment, contract length, effective date, index clause
  • Explicit supplier-health limit in strategic categories
  • A log of why each offer was made and why each deal was accepted

What does not, yet

  • Real suppliers are not deterministic and do not announce a final offer
  • The agent reads structure, not text: objections only register when they change a sheet
  • Fixed-clock timing rules would be wrong against a human
  • No memory across negotiations with the same supplier

Next step: a language model that turns an objection into a structured hypothesis (what they value, what they refuse), with the arithmetic and the accept checks staying deterministic.

Be honest here. The cost discipline and the cited evidence are the part a procurement team would keep. The timing tricks are arena-specific.
